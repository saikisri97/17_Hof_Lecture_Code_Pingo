# Question review — 20 November 2026

Ready with an access limitation: local files are prepared; publication and live Pingo import are untested.

The previous prompts followed the one-change experiment structure, but only partly followed notice → think → explain. Several were yes/no checks or broad definitions; Task 3 bundled bias, selection and final checking. Some distractors were unrelated to sales planning.

All five experiments now have an explicit observation, a question about meaning, and a short explanation to a colleague. Questions name the same runs and visible evidence. Student solutions answer each question in order. Pingo uses the same cases with plausible misconceptions. No new calculations, methods, inventory or transport decisions are required.

| Experiment | Notice | Think | Explain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ten unknowns remain | Zero would assert no sales | Ask a colleague to check source records |
| 2 | Reference versus adjusted plan | Assumption versus observed sales | Explain the promotion plan and check event details |
| 3 | MA3 versus MA6 validation errors | More history does not guarantee accuracy | Describe the separate final check without overstating improvement |
| 4 | Weekly quantity versus period total | Sales rate versus time covered | Give the four-week quantity with its assumption |
| 5 | Closer forecast at each actual outcome | Fixed forecasts, different outcome | Respond to an unsupported always-adjust claim |

Tasks 1 and 4 remain deliberately simple concept checks. Task 1 reveals details rather than changing a forecasting method; Task 4 repeats a weekly estimate rather than testing a multi-week model. Their prompts now state that meaning without adding a new mathematics task.

The slides retain the same tasks, parameters and results; this revision changes notebook prompts and Pingo, not the deck. Native PowerPoint and live classroom use were not tested.
