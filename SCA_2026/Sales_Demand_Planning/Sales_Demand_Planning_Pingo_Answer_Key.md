# Sales & Demand Planning — Pingo answers

One correct answer per question. Answer letters below follow XML order; Pingo may shuffle options.

## SD01 — Read the sales history
Task 1 reveals record details, but 10 weekly sales values remain unknown. A colleague wants to enter zero for all 10 before forecasting. What is the best reply?

**Answer B:** Keep them unknown and check the source: zero would claim no units were sold.

Unknown sales are not observed zero sales. Showing details does not recover the outcomes; check missing and unresolved source records.

## SD02 — Review a promotion assumption
In Task 2, Sales proposes a promotion next week. With the assumed uplift changed from 0% to 20%, SKU_A’s plan rises from 150.33 to 180.40 units. How should the planner describe 180.40?

**Answer C:** A plan conditional on the assumed 20% increase for next week’s promotion.

The original reference stays 150.33. The higher quantity comes from the stated future-promotion assumption; no future sales or accuracy improvement has been observed.

## SD03 — Compare forecast windows
In Task 3, SKU_A validation MAE is 20.83 units/week for MA3 and 36.54 for MA6 on the same 12 weeks. A colleague recommends MA6 because it uses more history. Which reply fits this run?

**Answer C:** MA3 has lower validation error here; a longer window did not improve accuracy.

MAE measures average absolute error; smaller is closer. MA3 is better on this validation comparison. The separate final check does not show an improvement over the previous-week benchmark.

## SD04 — Change the planning horizon
In Task 4, the four-week total is 601.33 units while the weekly estimate stays about 150.33 units. The sales manager asks why the total is larger. Which explanation is correct?

**Answer A:** We expect about 150.33 units each week and have added four weeks together.

The run extends the period covered by the total while keeping the weekly sales estimate constant. It does not apply the promotion adjustment or detect a change in sales rate.

## SD05 — Review a forecast against actual sales
In Task 5, the original forecast is closer at hypothetical actual sales of 150; the +20% forecast is closer at 190. Sales says the 190-unit example proves we should always add 20%. Which reply fits both runs?

**Answer D:** The adjustment helps in one example and hurts in the other; these outcomes do not justify always adding 20%.

At 150 actual units, original/adjusted absolute errors are 0.33/30.40; at 190 they are 39.67/9.60. Both forecasts stay fixed. These are hypothetical outcomes, not evidence of a causal promotion effect.
