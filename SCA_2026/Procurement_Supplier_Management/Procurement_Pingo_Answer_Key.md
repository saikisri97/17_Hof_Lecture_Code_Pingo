# Procurement — Pingo answer key

Answer letters follow XML order; options may shuffle in Pingo.

## PR01 — Compare delivery speed and spread
In Task 1, A averages 10.3 days with spread 0.91 days; B averages 9.0 days with spread 2.47 days. A buyer calls B both faster and more consistent. Which reply matches the run?

**Answer A:** B is faster on average, while A has more consistent timing.

The mean describes speed. Standard deviation and the histograms show B has wider timing variation. The sample sizes are the same.

## PR02 — Read delivery performance against a target
In Task 2, changing the internal target from 10 to 12 days raises A’s within-target percentage from 60% to 100%. Its promised-date OTD stays 90%. What should the buyer report?

**Answer C:** More unchanged deliveries meet the relaxed internal target; promise performance is unchanged.

Only the reporting target changes. Actual receipts and promised dates stay fixed; the two percentages answer different questions.

## PR03 — Compare two quality measures
Task 3 shows A has 12/60 flagged orders and 72/29,940 defective inspected units. B has 6/60 flagged orders and 180/29,980 defective units. Which interpretation is supported?

**Answer B:** B has fewer affected orders but a higher defective-unit proportion.

Flagged-order incidence is 20% for A and 10% for B. Defective-unit percentages are 0.2405% and 0.6004%. Orders and units are different denominators.

## PR04 — Compare purchase price and recorded cost
In Task 4, A has purchase price EUR 9.00/unit and recorded cost EUR 10.519/unit; B has EUR 10.00 and EUR 10.348. Which statement belongs in the procurement review?

**Answer D:** A has the lower purchase price, but B costs less across the recorded procurement categories.

The recorded categories include purchase, freight, inspection and rework per received unit. They exclude downtime, financing, administration and later lifecycle costs.

## PR05 — Screen suppliers and check capacity
In Task 5, tightening the defective-unit ceiling from 1.0% to 0.5% leaves only A eligible, with 8,000 July units against a 9,000-unit need. B’s defect rate stays 0.6004%. What follows?

**Answer C:** Review B’s quality issue and resolve the 1,000-unit capacity gap before committing the supply plan.

The rule changes eligibility, not quality or capacity. A alone cannot cover the requirement. A supplier follow-up needs evidence, agreed action and a comparable later review.
