# Lecture notes

Suggested pacing:90–110 minutes including 25–35 minutes hands-on and discussion. This is an estimate, not a classroom trial.

Concept focus: delivery speed versus consistency, order and unit quality denominators, cost boundaries, qualification, capacity and supplier follow-up.

## Task 1: Compare delivery speed and spread
Run SUPPLIER: 'A' → 'B'.
Teaching purpose: A buyer says, “B arrives faster, so its timing is more predictable.” Do both parts follow from these outputs?
Expected explanation: “B averages 9.0 days, but its timing varies more: spread is 2.47 days versus A’s 0.91. We should discuss both average speed and consistency when reviewing delivery performance.”

## Task 2: Read delivery performance against a target
Run TARGET_DAYS: 10 → 12.
Teaching purpose: The reported target percentage improves. Have any suppliers actually delivered sooner?
Expected explanation: “We changed the internal target from 10 to 12 days, so more orders meet it. Delivery dates and performance against the original promised dates have not changed.”

## Task 3: Compare two quality measures
Run QUALITY_VIEW: 'orders' → 'units'.
Teaching purpose: Why can B have fewer flagged orders but a larger defective-unit percentage? Use the counts in the table.
Expected explanation: “B has quality incidents on fewer orders, but those incidents contain more defective units overall. We should report both the frequency of affected orders and the defective-unit percentage.”

## Task 4: Compare purchase price and recorded cost
Run COST_BASIS: 'purchase' → 'recorded'.
Teaching purpose: What included costs explain why the ranking changes?
Expected explanation: “A has the lowest purchase price, while B has the lowest cost across the recorded categories. We have not included downtime, financing, administration or later lifecycle costs, so this is not a complete total-cost-of-ownership comparison.”

## Task 5: Screen suppliers and check capacity
Run MAX_DEFECTIVE_PCT: 1.0 → 0.5.
Teaching purpose: Did the stricter quality ceiling change supplier quality or the buyer’s acceptance rule?
Expected explanation: “B exceeds the stricter 0.5% ceiling, so procurement should review the defect records, agree a corrective action and track its defective-unit percentage. A alone offers 8,000 units against a need of 9,000, so the buyer must resolve the 1,000-unit gap before committing the sourcing plan.”

## Facilitation boundaries
Use the status counts before interpreting completed-order OTD. Keep the July screening rules explicit and hypothetical. Do not infer root causes from aggregate differences or treat supplier C’s conditional status as a legal certification claim. The segmentation matrix needs market and business-impact information beyond the case.
